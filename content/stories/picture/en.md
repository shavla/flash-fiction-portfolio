He picked up the glass from the table and raised it to his lips. A few drops trickled down his salt-and-pepper beard and fell to the floor.

"I have never been so miserable, so defeated, so utterly buried."

He thought, growing angry with himself.

"Why?"

There was barely enough left in the bottle for one or two more glasses, so he didn't bother pouring another. He drank straight from the bottle. As he tilted his head back, he caught sight of the ceiling, illuminated by moonlight reflected from the floor. He seemed to commit the image to memory, yet only one thing kept circling in his mind.

Only her big, green eyes were sad and full of tears.

For a brief moment, he forgot everything. He could feel the dance again, vivid and bright, to music from "God Help the Girl." He had never imagined that such a wonderful time could ever come to an end.

He took her photograph from the pocket over his heart. It was folded into four. He looked at it, then tucked it away again, because the vision of her in his imagination seemed more beautiful. It was as if he could even smell her fragrance.

Only her eyes remained as sad and tearful as ever.

And yet, at the same time, he felt a sense of joy, believing that wherever the person in the photograph was, she must be happy there. With one hand, he took the photograph out once more. He looked at it.

"Oh, how beautiful you are!" he thought, as the last sip from the bottle slipped down his throat.

A faint smile played across his face, and as he tilted his head back, he surrendered to death.

The only sound in the room was the dull thud of the bottle hitting the floor. The photograph was still in his hand.
