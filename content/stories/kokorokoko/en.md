And I can hear footsteps. It's getting closer. I don't know what it looks like; I've never seen it before. But it reeks of death. The kind of death that comes without warning and never turns back. It's not going anywhere. It's come to take me.

Everything was a lie. One lie piled upon another. They used to say life was beautiful, but I never saw anything beautiful about it. Many times during mealtime, I've watched one of our own being put to death. It was always done with that terrible, razor-sharp blade, one swing of which was enough to sever an entire neck. Then they would start flapping about, as if trying to run away, but you can't get far without a head. Perhaps they don't know that, since they no longer have heads on their necks.

I used to wonder whether I would do the same, or whether my lame limp would hold me back, making life even more miserable than it already was. I can't remember a single time when I managed to beat any of my peers to the call of "chick, chick, chick!" I was always the last to reach the feed, and the last to leave.

But now everything is drawing closer. It's nearer than ever.

Perhaps I would never have found myself in this situation if they hadn't taken me from the yard to another village, where they plucked the feathers from my wings. And yet, they seemed like good people. They even treated me with such care. I was already defective below the waist, and now I'm defective above it, too. First Mother Nature abandoned me, and then humans condemned me. The earth refused to take me in, and the sky betrayed me.

It's getting closer, and the bushes are rustling.

The bushes tremble, and in the moonlight, gleaming white teeth and two yellow eyes appear before me.

Farewell, you chickens nestled warmly in your coop. It's here.
