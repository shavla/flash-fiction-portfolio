---
title: The Last Train
date: 2026-10-28
excerpt: Every night at 23:40 the old man bought a ticket he never used.
---

Every night at 23:40 the old man bought a ticket for the last train to the sea.

The cashier, a young woman with tired eyes, stopped asking after the first month. She simply slid the ticket under the glass, and he paid with exact change — coins still warm from his pocket.

He never got on. He sat on the bench beneath the station clock, watched the doors close, watched the red lights grow small in the dark, and then he walked home.

One night she closed her window early and sat down beside him.

"Who are you waiting for?" she asked.

He looked at the ticket in his hand as if seeing it for the first time.

"Forty years ago," he said, "someone asked me to meet her on this train. I was late. Only five minutes." He smiled. "I keep thinking that one night, the train will be late too."

***

The next evening the train left at 23:40, as always.

But the bench was empty. And on the cashier's window hung a small handwritten note: *Closed. Gone to the sea.*
